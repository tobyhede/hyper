# A lane is offset along its curve

Status: accepted
Refines: 0100

ADR 0100 drew each lane as the lone Edge's curve moved sideways whole: both anchors moved by one vector along the side of the Resource they sit on, and `getBezierPath` over the moved anchors answered the same curve, translated. A lane is now the lone curve **offset along its own normal**: at every point it runs the lane's distance from the Active Graph's Edge, measured square to the curve at that point. The lone curve is still `getBezierPath` over the unmoved anchors, so React Flow remains the one author of its shape, and the offset is computed from the cubic read back from its path.

**Why the translate was dropped.** A translate keeps its distance only where the curve runs square to the direction it was moved. Where the curve makes an angle θ with the facing axis, the gap seen between a lane and the Edge beside it is the spacing times |cos θ|, so it closes as the curve turns toward the direction of the move. Between Resources far apart across the facing axis — stacked far apart and joined through left and right anchors, or side by side far apart and joined through top and bottom — the middle of the curve runs nearly along the move, and the lanes close to almost nothing there. With a 3-unit stroke on the Active Graph's Edge and 2-unit strokes on the others, the visible gap at the 8-unit spacing is about 8·cos θ − 2.5, so neighbouring lines touch once the curve is steeper than about 72°. ADR 0100 wanted several Graphs between one pair to read as one connection carried by several Graphs; lines that drift into each other in the middle read instead as Graphs that cross.

**How the offset is drawn.** The offset of a curved cubic is not itself a cubic, so a lane is a run of cubics joined end to end. Each is Tiller and Hanson's offset of a piece of the lone curve: every leg of the piece's control polygon moved along its own normal by the distance, adjacent moved legs crossed to give the inner control points, and the ends moved along the curve's end normals. A piece whose offset strays from the true offset is halved and each half offset in turn, to a fixed depth. A straight Edge needs one piece, and its lanes are the same straight lines beside it that ADR 0100 drew.

**The offset is drawn over the stretch of the curve where it stays a copy, and bridged to the anchors outside it.** An offset stays a copy of the curve only where it bends no tighter than twice the lane's distance; inside a bend tighter than the distance the offset doubles back into a swallowtail, which between Resources close together is drawn over the source Resource, and inside one nearly that tight it turns a sharp corner. React Flow's curve bends tightest right at its anchors whenever the target is only a little ahead of the source and far across it, so a rule asking this of the whole curve lost the offset exactly where the translate closes worst. So `laneBezier` finds the **span**: the widest stretch around the curve's midpoint over which the curve bends no tighter than twice the pair's **reach** — how far out its outermost lane runs — and at whose ends the offsets at the reach either side lie ahead of the anchors along the facing axis. Over the span the lane is the offset. Outside it each end is one cubic **bridge** from the lane's own point beside its anchor, leaving along the side's facing direction, to the offset at the span's end, arriving along the offset's direction there; its handles are shortened so its control points run forwards along the facing axis, so the bridge runs forwards and never past the curve's ends. Where the curve bends gently enough all along, the span is the whole curve and the lane is the offset with no bridges. The span is decided from the curve and the reach alone, so every lane of a pair lands at the same two points and the lanes, offsets on one normal between them, never cross: a span per lane was prototyped and an outer lane's bridge cut across an inner lane's offset. Only where there is no span is a lane the translate ADR 0100 drew — both anchors moved by one vector along their sides and `getBezierPath` over the moved anchors — for the whole pair: where the curve turns back against the direction it leaves in, as between Resources whose target sits behind the source, and the offset of each turn reaches past the curve's own extent; where it does not leave and arrive along the direction its sides face, as where the anchors are level on the facing axis and React Flow puts each control point on its anchor; and where the anchors are so close along the facing axis and far apart across it that even at the midpoint an offset at the reach would sit behind a Resource. So spacing is constant over the span wherever there is one, where the bridges are shorter than a detached lane’s trim what shows of it runs entirely at its spacing, and nowhere is a lane drawn nearer its neighbour than the translate drew it — measured by a property over both axes and directions rather than proved.

**Lanes remain parallel, not fanned.** An offset is the strict sense of parallel — equidistant everywhere — which the translate only approximated. Nothing here bows a lane by curvature or lets lanes meet at the ends; ADR 0100's rejection of fanning stands.

**The ends and arrowheads do not move.** At a left, right, top or bottom anchor React Flow's curve leaves along the direction the side faces, so the curve's normal at an anchor lies along the side, and an offset puts each end of a lane exactly where a translate does: beside its anchor, moved along the side the anchor sits on. Arrowheads land where they did. The sign convention is unchanged — positive below an Edge whose sides are left and right, to the right of one whose sides are top and bottom, whichever way it travels. The label sits at the offset of the lone curve's midpoint, or on a translated lane at the translated midpoint. The trim still cuts the lane rather than moving its ends, measured along the lane so both ends lose the same length.

**Self-Edges are unchanged.** A self-Edge's two sides are on different axes, so its lanes were never a translate: each anchor moves along its own side away from the corner, and the loop between them is `getBezierPath` over the moved anchors. ADR 0100 already accepts that those lanes may cross.

What this does not touch: which Graph takes which lane, the spacing, the strokes, the trim fraction, and the anchors and their facing rule (ADR 0087). The change is inside `laneBezier`, except that each lane now also carries its pair's reach (`GraphLane.reach`, projected as the Edge's `laneReach`), because only `graphLanes` sees the whole pair.
